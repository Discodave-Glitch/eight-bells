From the maker of Slipway

Eight Bells

Euclidean beats and melodies for the M-VAVE FM-1 and its custom firmwares. Up to 8 tracks depending on the firmware, up to 64 steps each. It can play the pattern on the FM-1 itself over USB MIDI.

Before you start

Eight Bells makes patterns for an M-VAVE FM-1 running custom firmware, or the official M-VAVE firmware. Pick yours in Play on the FM-1 so the track layout, channels and notes match. If your FM-1 still has the stock firmware, install a custom one first. Slipway is a Windows app that lists custom FM-1 firmware and installs it for you, and a few firmwares have their own web flasher instead. Close Slipway, the firmware's web editor and the M-VAVE workbench before you connect here, because only one app can use the FM-1's MIDI port at a time.

  • Slipway on GitHub, the app that installs FM-1 firmware
  • FM-1 firmware catalogue, the list Slipway reads
  • The Firmware Hub, another list of FM-1 firmware
  • Felucca on GitHub, the firmware and installer that Slipway uses

Every firmware's card in Play on the FM-1 links to its own release page. Installing firmware is at your own risk. Save the stock firmware with Slipway before you change anything, so you can put it back.

Play on the FM-1

MIDI is not connected. Plug in the FM-1 with a USB data cable, switch it on, then tap Connect.

Browse all firmwares

The same community catalogue that Slipway reads. Pick one to apply its MIDI settings. Install firmware with Slipway, or with the flasher on the firmware's own page where it has one. The people who made each firmware are credited on its card. The Firmware Hub lists most of them as open source under GPL-3.0 and FM-1+VA as not open source, so check each project's own licence.

MIDI monitor: does your FM-1 send notes?

Tap Connect MIDI, choose the FM-1 as the input and tap Listen. Then start a pattern on the FM-1, or play its keys. If notes appear here, the firmware is sending them over USB. On Sloop, the sequencer only sends its notes when GLO, then MIDI 2, then OUT is set to SEQ. Otherwise only the keys you press are sent.

Not listening. Choose the FM-1 as the input, then tap Listen and start the pattern on the FM-1.


        

        

          Capture a loop played by the FM-1
          
Turns a loop on the FM-1 into tracks here, which you can edit and share as an exact pattern code. On Sloop, first set GLO, then MIDI 2, then OUT to SEQ so the sequencer sends its notes. Without a clock, so the app treats the first note it hears as step 1. Set the app tempo (under Generate) to exactly the FM-1 tempo first, and pick the same number of bars as the loop. Each capture only replaces the tracks it hears, so you can capture a track at a time.

Punch effects (Sloop)
      
Sloop plays its 27 punch effects from MIDI on notes F3 to G5. On the FM-1, go to GLO, then MIDI 3, and set FX CH to a free channel, for example 11. Tap Connect MIDI in Play on the FM-1 first. Choose the same channel here, then hold a button to run that effect on the whole mix. Other firmwares don't publish this, so the panel is hidden for them.

Sound controls (optional)
        
Sends MIDI controllers to the FM-1 while it plays, so you can shape each track without touching the knobs. Only sliders you move are sent. Filter: below the middle is low-pass, above is high-pass.

        

        

      

    

      
      
    

    

    
Tapping a step edits

  

  
Tap a step to add or remove a hit, or switch to Velocity and tap a hit to cycle soft, medium and loud. The bar in each step shows how hard it plays. Lock a track to keep it while the rest regenerate. Hits, Steps and Rotate rebuild a track as a Euclidean pattern. If the Sloop editor shows notes an octave off, flip Middle C.



  
Track colours
    
Tracks 1 to 4 start in the same colours as the Sloop screen: blue, green, yellow and orange. Tap a swatch to change any track's colour. Your choices are remembered in this browser.
